# Everyday nutrition for badminton players

General guidance for healthy players. People with medical conditions, eating disorders or specific weight
goals should work with a registered dietitian or doctor.

## Energy: eat enough for training
Badminton training burns a lot of energy. Under-eating (low energy availability) can lead to fatigue, poor
recovery, frequent illness, injuries and, in female athletes, menstrual disruption. Regular meals and snacks
built around whole foods are the foundation; supplements cannot make up for a poor diet.

## Carbohydrates: the main fuel
Carbohydrates are the main fuel for high-intensity rallies. As a guide from sports nutrition position
statements, players doing about an hour per day of moderate training need roughly **5-7 g of carbohydrate per
kg of body weight per day**, lower on rest days and higher on very heavy training days. Good sources are rice,
roti/chapati, oats, millets, potatoes, fruit, poha, idli/dosa and whole-grain bread. For a 60 kg player, 5 g/kg
is about 300 g of carbohydrate per day.

## Protein: repair and adaptation
Protein supports muscle repair and adaptation to training. The ISSN position stand suggests about **1.4-2.0 g of
protein per kg of body weight per day** for most exercising people, spread over the day in meals of roughly
20-40 g (about 0.25-0.4 g/kg per meal). Sources include eggs, chicken, fish, milk, curd/yogurt, paneer, tofu,
soy chunks, lentils (dal), chickpeas, rajma and other legumes. A 60 kg player would aim for roughly 85-120 g per
day.

## Fats, vitamins and minerals
Healthy fats (nuts, seeds, oils, fish) support overall health. Micronutrients of special interest for athletes
include **iron** (oxygen transport; higher risk of deficiency in female and vegetarian athletes), **calcium and
vitamin D** (bone health) and **magnesium** (muscle function). A varied diet with fruit, vegetables, dairy or
fortified alternatives, legumes and whole grains usually covers needs; blood tests and supplements should be
guided by a doctor.

## Building a balanced plate
A simple plate model for main meals: about half vegetables and fruit, a quarter carbohydrate (more on heavy
training days), a quarter protein, plus a small portion of healthy fat. On rest days, slightly reduce the
carbohydrate portion while keeping protein steady.
