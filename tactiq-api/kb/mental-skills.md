# Mental skills and match routines

## Between-point routines
Badminton has many short breaks between rallies. A consistent routine between points (for example: turn away,
take one slow breath, adjust strings, decide the next serve or return plan, then step in) helps players reset
after mistakes and play the next point with a clear mind instead of dwelling on the last one.

## Managing nerves before matches
Pre-match nerves are normal. Helpful strategies: a fixed warm-up routine, slow breathing (for example inhale for
4 seconds and exhale for 6 seconds, repeated for a minute), focusing on process goals ("move early, keep
length") instead of outcome goals ("I must win"), and reminding yourself of good preparation.

## Handling a losing streak within a match
When the opponent wins several points in a row, use the allowed time to slow the tempo, return to high-percentage
shots (deep clears, safe serves), and change one tactical element at a time (for example play more to the
opponent's backhand). Avoid trying risky winners out of frustration.

## Reviewing matches constructively
After each match, note one or two things that went well, one thing to improve, and any patterns in how points
were lost. Logging a short note and a self-rating in TactiQ after every match turns these reflections into
trends over time and gives the AI coach context for advice.
