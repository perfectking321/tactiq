# Singles tactics

## Core idea of singles
Singles is a game of **space and time**. The aim is to move the opponent to the four corners so that a gap
opens, or to force a weak reply that can be attacked. Patience matters: most amateur singles points are won
by the opponent's errors, so consistent length (deep clears, tight net shots) usually beats risky winners.

## Using the four corners
A classic pattern is to play length to the rear and then drop or net to the front corner on the opposite
side, making the opponent cover the diagonal (the longest distance on court). Playing straight and then
cross-court tests the opponent's movement. Hitting repeatedly to the same corner can also work if the opponent
anticipates a change and moves early.

## When to attack
Attack (smash or fast drop) when the shuttle is in front of you, high, and you are balanced. Smashing from
deep in the rear court or off balance often gives the opponent an easy block and leaves you out of position.
In singles a smash should be followed by quick forward movement to cover the net reply.

## Reading the opponent
Watch for patterns: Do they always lift from the net? Is their backhand rear corner weak? Do they rush the net
after serving? Keep simple notes after each match (for example in the TactiQ match log) about what worked and
what did not; patterns become clear over several matches.

## Fitness and pacing in singles
Singles rallies are long and repeated with short rests, so stamina strongly affects the third game. If a
player tires in the third game, the training plan should include high-intensity interval work and match
practice in addition to technique. Between rallies, use the allowed time to breathe slowly and reset.
