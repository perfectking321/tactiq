# Net play, drives and defence

## Net shots
A net shot (or "net drop") is played from near the net so the shuttle tumbles just over the tape and falls
close to the net on the other side. The key is to take the shuttle as **high and early** as possible, with a
soft grip and a gentle push or slice. A **spinning (tumbling) net shot** makes the shuttle turn over at the
top, which is hard to return cleanly. Reach with the racket arm and lunge with the racket-side leg, keeping
the racket head up.

## Net kill and push
If the opponent's shot floats above the net tape, the **net kill** punishes it with a short, sharp downward
tap. It uses a small backswing; a big swing risks hitting the net or committing a fault. A **push** is a flat
shot from the front or mid-court into the opponent's mid-court gaps, often used in doubles.

## The lift
The lift is an underarm shot from the front court that sends the shuttle high to the opponent's back court.
It is mostly a defensive shot used when the player cannot attack, so it must be high and deep enough to give
time to recover. A short lift invites a smash. An **attacking lift** is lower and faster to catch an opponent
off balance.

## Drives
Drives are flat, fast shots that travel parallel to the floor across the net, usually from the mid-court
sides. They are common in doubles. Use a compact swing, short backswing, and a quick reset of the racket in
front of the body. Forehand and backhand drive exchanges are a good drill for racket speed and reaction.

## Defending against smashes
In defence, stand slightly lower and wider with the racket in front of the body, often with a backhand grip
since most body smashes are best covered on the backhand side. Common replies are the **block** (soft return
to the net), the **drive** (flat counter-attack) and the **lift** (high, deep defence). A good defensive block
uses the pace of the smash rather than swinging. Watching the opponent's racket and staying relaxed is
more important than speed of swing.
