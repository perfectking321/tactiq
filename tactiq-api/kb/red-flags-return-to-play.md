# Red flags, seeing a professional, and returning to play

## Stop and seek urgent medical help
Stop playing immediately and seek urgent medical care (emergency services if severe) for:
- Chest pain or pressure, fainting or near-fainting, unusual breathlessness or an irregular racing heartbeat
  during exercise.
- A blow to the head followed by headache, confusion, dizziness, vomiting or memory problems (possible
  concussion; do not return to play the same day).
- A visible deformity, a bone that may be broken, or inability to put any weight on the leg.
- A "pop" or snapping sensation with sudden pain and rapid swelling in a joint, or a feeling of being kicked in
  the back of the heel.
- Numbness, tingling or weakness in an arm or leg, or loss of bladder/bowel control with back pain.
- Severe heat illness signs: confusion, very high body temperature, stopping sweating.

## See a physiotherapist or doctor soon
Book an assessment when:
- Pain lasts more than about a week despite rest or keeps returning every time you play.
- Pain is severe (roughly 7/10 or higher), wakes you at night, or makes you limp.
- A joint feels unstable, locks or gives way.
- Swelling does not settle within a few days.
An AI coach can give general information but cannot examine an injury; it should never replace a qualified
professional.

## Training around a minor niggle
For mild discomfort (roughly 0-3/10) that settles within 24 hours, many players can continue modified training,
avoiding the movements that clearly provoke pain and keeping other fitness going (for example upper-body work
with an ankle niggle, or cycling with a sore shoulder). Pain that increases during the session or is worse the
next morning means the load was too high.

## Returning to play after injury
Return to play should be gradual and criteria-based, not just based on time. A typical progression is: pain-free
daily activities, then strength and balance close to the uninjured side, then straight-line running, then
badminton footwork and lunges, then non-competitive hitting, then practice matches, and finally competition.
Each stage should be completed without pain increasing the next day. Decisions for significant injuries should
be shared with a physiotherapist or doctor.
