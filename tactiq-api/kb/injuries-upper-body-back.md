# Shoulder, elbow and lower-back problems in badminton

Important: this is general education, not a diagnosis. Persistent or severe pain needs assessment by a
physiotherapist or doctor.

## Shoulder pain in overhead players
Repeated overhead strokes (clears and smashes) load the rotator cuff and the tissues around the shoulder.
Gradual pain on the outside or front of the shoulder when smashing or reaching overhead is common. Contributing
factors include sudden increases in smash practice, weak rotator cuff and shoulder-blade muscles, and technique
that relies on the arm instead of the legs and trunk. Management usually includes reducing overhead hitting
volume for a period, strengthening the rotator cuff and upper back (external rotations, rows), and correcting
technique. Seek assessment for pain at night, significant weakness, or a feeling the shoulder slips out.

## Elbow and forearm pain
Pain on the outer elbow (lateral elbow tendinopathy, "tennis elbow") can occur in badminton from repetitive
backhand shots, gripping too tightly, or an unsuitable grip size or string tension. Relax the grip between
shots, check equipment, and strengthen the forearm gradually. Pain on the inner elbow may be related to forehand
overuse. Persistent elbow pain should be reviewed by a professional.

## Wrist
Wrist pain can come from heavy use of the wrist instead of forearm rotation, or from falls. Swelling, pain on
the thumb side of the wrist after a fall, or inability to grip must be checked for a fracture.

## Lower back
Badminton combines bending, lunging and trunk rotation, which can load the lower back. Most back pain in
players is non-specific and improves with staying active, adjusting training, and trunk and hip strengthening.
However, back pain with **leg numbness or weakness, loss of bladder or bowel control, numbness around the
groin, fever, or pain after a significant fall** is a red flag that needs urgent medical attention.
