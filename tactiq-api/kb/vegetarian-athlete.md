# Vegetarian, vegan and eggetarian players

## Meeting protein needs without meat
Vegetarian and vegan players can fully meet their protein needs by planning meals. Because many plant proteins
contain less of some essential amino acids (such as leucine and lysine) and are digested slightly less
efficiently, it helps to aim for the upper part of the recommended range and to combine sources across the day,
for example cereals with legumes (rice with dal, roti with chana).

## Good protein sources by diet type
- **Vegetarian (lacto):** milk, curd/yogurt, paneer, cheese, plus all plant sources.
- **Eggetarian:** all of the above plus eggs (about 6 g protein per egg).
- **Vegan:** tofu, tempeh, soy chunks, soy milk, lentils/dal, chickpeas, rajma, moong, peanuts, nuts and seeds,
  and whole grains.
Approximate protein: 100 g paneer about 18 g; 100 g firm tofu about 12-15 g; 1 cup cooked dal about 15-18 g;
50 g dry soy chunks about 25 g; 1 cup milk about 8 g.

## Example high-protein vegetarian day
Breakfast: oats cooked in milk with peanuts and banana, or besan chilla with curd. Lunch: rice/roti, dal, a
paneer or chana dish and salad. Snack: roasted chana or sprouts chaat, or a glass of milk. Dinner: roti, rajma or
tofu curry, vegetables, curd. After training: lassi or soy milk smoothie with fruit.

## Nutrients to watch
- **Vitamin B12:** not found reliably in plant foods; vegans need fortified foods or a supplement, and many
  vegetarians benefit from checking levels with a doctor.
- **Iron:** plant iron is absorbed less well; combine iron-rich foods (legumes, green leafy vegetables, jaggery,
  fortified cereals) with vitamin C sources (lemon, amla, citrus) and avoid tea or coffee right with meals.
- **Calcium and vitamin D:** from dairy or fortified plant milks; sunlight for vitamin D.
- **Omega-3 fats:** flaxseed, chia and walnuts provide plant omega-3.
