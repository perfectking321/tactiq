# Pre-match, tournament-day and recovery nutrition

## The pre-match meal (2-4 hours before)
Eat a familiar, carbohydrate-rich meal with moderate protein and low fat and fibre 2-4 hours before playing, so
it is digested by match time. Position statements suggest around **1-4 g of carbohydrate per kg** in the hours
before exercise, with the larger amounts when there is more time to digest. Examples: rice with dal and a little
curd; idli with sambar; oats with milk and banana; a sandwich with eggs or paneer. Avoid heavy, oily, very spicy
or new foods on match day.

## Snack before playing (30-60 minutes)
If hungry close to playing, have a small, easily digested carbohydrate snack: a banana, a few dates, a small
bowl of poha, crackers, or a sports drink. Avoid large amounts of protein or fat right before play.

## Between matches at a tournament
Tournament days can involve several matches with uncertain gaps. Plan ahead and carry food:
- **Gap under 1 hour:** fluids and quick carbohydrate (fruit, sports drink, glucose biscuits, dates).
- **Gap of 1-2 hours:** a light snack such as a banana with a few nuts, a sandwich, or curd with fruit.
- **Gap over 2 hours:** a small meal similar to the pre-match meal.
Sip fluids regularly and keep checking urine colour.

## Recovery after training or matches
To refuel and repair, eat within roughly 1-2 hours after playing, especially when there is another session
within 24 hours. Aim for carbohydrate plus about **20-40 g of protein** (around 0.25-0.4 g/kg). Examples: milk
or a curd/lassi with fruit; chicken or paneer with rice and vegetables; dal-rice with an egg; a smoothie with
milk, banana and oats. When two sessions are close together (for example the same day), higher carbohydrate
intake soon after the first session matters more.

## Caffeine and supplements
Moderate caffeine (for example coffee or tea) can improve alertness and performance for some adults, but it can
disturb sleep and cause jitters; teenagers should be cautious. Supplements such as creatine have evidence in
some sports, but players should only use tested products, avoid anything promising rapid results, and talk to
a doctor or dietitian first, particularly if they are under 18 or compete in drug-tested events.
