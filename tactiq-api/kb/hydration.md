# Hydration for training and matches

## Why hydration matters in badminton
Indoor halls can be hot and humid, and players sweat heavily during long sessions. Losing more than about 2% of
body weight through sweat can impair endurance, concentration and decision-making. Over-drinking is also
possible and can be dangerous, so the goal is to replace losses sensibly, not to drink as much as possible.

## Before playing
Start sessions well hydrated. A common guideline is to drink about **5-7 ml per kg of body weight** around 4
hours before exercise (about 300-420 ml for a 60 kg player), and a little more about 2 hours before if urine is
still dark. Pale-yellow urine is a simple sign of good hydration.

## During play
Sip fluids at breaks between games and during intervals rather than drinking large amounts at once. For sessions
under about an hour, water is usually enough. For longer sessions, tournaments or very heavy sweating, a sports
drink or water with some salt and sugar (an electrolyte drink) helps replace sodium and provides carbohydrate.
Players who sweat a lot or get cramps may need more sodium.

## After play: estimate your sweat loss
Weigh yourself before and after a typical session (minimal clothing, after towelling off). Each 1 kg of weight
lost is roughly 1 litre of sweat. To rehydrate, drink about **1.25-1.5 litres for every kg lost** over the next
few hours, along with a meal or snack containing some salt.

## Daily fluid intake
Daily needs vary with body size, climate and training. Many active people need around 2.5-3.5 litres of total
fluid per day, more in hot weather and on heavy training days. Tracking daily water intake in the TactiQ check-in
helps spot days of low intake, especially around exams or travel.
