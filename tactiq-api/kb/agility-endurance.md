# Agility, speed and match endurance

## The physical demands of badminton
Badminton is a high-intensity intermittent sport: short, very intense rallies with rapid accelerations,
decelerations and changes of direction, separated by short rests between points. Players need a good aerobic
base to recover between rallies and games, and anaerobic power for explosive movement. Training should
therefore mix endurance and short, intense intervals rather than only long slow running.

## Building stamina (aerobic base)
Aerobic fitness helps the player recover between rallies and stay sharp in the third game. Options include
20-40 minutes of continuous jogging, cycling or skipping at a conversational pace one or two times a week,
especially in the off-season. For players short on time, longer on-court drills at moderate intensity also
develop endurance.

## Badminton-specific interval training
Intervals should resemble rallies. Examples:
- **Shadow footwork intervals:** 20-30 seconds of six-corner movement, 20-30 seconds rest, 8-12 repetitions.
- **Multi-shuttle sets:** 20-40 seconds at high intensity, rest at least as long as the work, 6-10 sets.
- **Court sprints / shuttle runs:** short sprints across the court width with turns.
Keep the quality of movement high; when footwork becomes sloppy, end the set.

## Agility and reaction drills
Agility combines quick feet with reacting to a signal. Use reaction-based shadow footwork (a partner points to
a corner at random), ladder drills for foot speed, and split-step drills where the player reacts to a coach's
racket or a light. Reaction drills are best done early in a session when the player is fresh.

## How to improve stamina for the third game
If a player tires late in matches, combine: one aerobic session per week, one or two interval sessions, and
regular full-length practice games. Check that sleep, hydration and carbohydrate intake are adequate, because
low energy availability often looks like poor fitness.
