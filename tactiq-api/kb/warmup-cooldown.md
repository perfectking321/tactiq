# Warm-up and cool-down

## Why warm up
A warm-up raises body and muscle temperature, increases heart rate gradually, and prepares joints, tendons and
the nervous system for fast, explosive movement. Structured warm-up programmes are a common part of injury
prevention strategies in racket sports. Skipping the warm-up and going straight into smashes or full-speed
footwork is a common cause of muscle strains, particularly of the calf, hamstring and shoulder.

## A simple 10-15 minute badminton warm-up
1. **Raise (3-5 min):** light jogging, side-steps, skipping or easy shadow footwork.
2. **Activate and mobilise (3-5 min):** dynamic movements such as leg swings, walking lunges, hip openers,
   arm circles, trunk rotations, and light resistance-band shoulder rotations.
3. **Potentiate (3-5 min):** short accelerations, split-step jumps, quick lunges to the corners, and gradually
   increasing shuttle hitting from gentle clears and drives to full-power smashes.
Static stretching held for a long time immediately before explosive play is not ideal; keep the warm-up
dynamic.

## Shoulder warm-up for overhead players
Because overhead strokes repeatedly load the shoulder, include a few sets of light band external and internal
rotations and arm swings, then progress hitting intensity. Players with a history of shoulder pain should
spend extra time here.

## Cool-down
After training or matches, take 5-10 minutes of easy movement (walking, light jogging) to bring heart rate
down, then gentle static stretching for the calves, hamstrings, quadriceps, hip flexors, chest and shoulders.
Cool-down is also a good moment to rehydrate and start the recovery meal or snack.
