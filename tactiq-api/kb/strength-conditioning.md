# Strength and conditioning for badminton

## Why strength training helps badminton players
Badminton demands repeated lunges, jumps, landings and fast changes of direction. Stronger legs and trunk
let a player push off faster and absorb landings with better control. Strength work for the posterior chain
(hamstrings, glutes), the calves and the rotator cuff is often recommended in badminton injury prevention
research, alongside better lunging and landing technique. Strength training, done with good form and
sensible progression, is safe for adolescents and adults.

## Key lower-body exercises
- **Squats** (goblet squat, then barbell squat for experienced players) for overall leg strength.
- **Split squats and reverse lunges** to mirror the badminton lunge on one leg.
- **Romanian deadlifts and hamstring bridges / Nordic hamstring curls** for the hamstrings.
- **Calf raises** (straight and bent knee) for Achilles and calf resilience.
- **Lateral lunges and step-ups** for side-to-side strength.
Start with 2-3 sets of 8-12 controlled repetitions, two sessions per week.

## Upper body and shoulder
- **Band external/internal rotations** and **side-lying external rotations** for the rotator cuff.
- **Rows** and **face pulls** to balance the pushing muscles and support shoulder posture.
- **Push-ups** and light overhead pressing for general strength.
- **Wrist and forearm** work (wrist curls, pronation/supination with a light weight) for grip endurance.

## Core and trunk
Rotation and anti-rotation exercises (pallof press, side planks, dead bugs, medicine-ball rotational throws)
help transfer force from the legs to the racket and protect the lower back.

## Plyometrics and speed
Once basic strength is established, add jumping and landing drills: squat jumps, lateral bounds, split-squat
jumps and drop landings with soft knees. Focus on landing quietly with knees tracking over the toes. Keep
volume low (for example 3-4 sets of 5 jumps) and stop when quality drops. Beginners and players with knee or
ankle pain should build basic strength first.
