# Doubles tactics and rotation

## Attack and defence formations
Doubles teams switch between two basic formations. In **attack**, partners play **front-and-back**: the rear
player smashes and drops while the front player covers the net and kills loose replies. In **defence**,
partners stand **side-by-side**, each covering one half of the court against smashes. The team should move
into attack whenever it hits downwards and into defence whenever it is forced to lift.

## Rotation
Rotation is how partners move between formations without leaving gaps. When a team lifts, the player who
lifted moves to the side that matches the direction of the lift and the partner fills the other side. When a
team gets an attacking chance, the player best placed moves to the rear to smash and the other moves to the
net. Simple communication ("mine", "yours", "back") avoids collisions and gaps down the middle.

## Serve and serve-return in doubles
Doubles is dominated by the first three shots. The low serve is standard; the receiver stands close to the
front service line and tries to attack any serve that rises above the tape with a push, net kill or fast
drive. The server, after serving, should be ready to cover the net immediately. Practise serve and return
as a pair, not only individually.

## Mixed doubles basics
In traditional mixed doubles the woman often covers the front court and the man the rear court, but modern
mixed pairs adapt to the strengths of each player. The key principles of front-and-back attack and side-by-side
defence still apply.

## Common doubles mistakes
- Both players watching the shuttle and leaving the middle open.
- Lifting too often instead of driving or blocking.
- The front player standing too far back and missing net kills.
- Not talking: agreeing who takes shots down the middle prevents many lost points.
