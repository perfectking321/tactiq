# Planning a training week and monitoring load

## Balancing a badminton week
A balanced week for an amateur or college player mixes four ingredients: **technique and footwork**,
**match play or game situations**, **physical conditioning** (strength, speed, endurance) and **recovery**.
Hard days should be followed by easier days. At least one full rest day per week is recommended, and two for
beginners or during exam periods. Avoid scheduling heavy leg strength work the day before a match.

## Example weeks by playing level
- **Beginner (2-3 days/week):** two court sessions focused on grips, basic strokes, serve and simple footwork,
  plus one short full-body strength session. Mostly low to moderate intensity.
- **Intermediate (3-5 days/week):** two to three technique/footwork sessions, one to two match-play sessions,
  two short strength or conditioning sessions (can follow court work), one easy recovery day.
- **Advanced (5-6 days/week):** specific sessions for footwork speed, multi-shuttle intensity, tactics and
  match play; two strength sessions; planned lighter weeks every 3-5 weeks.
These are starting points; adjust to the player's goal, academic schedule and how they feel.

## Session-RPE: measuring training load without devices
Session-RPE is a simple, well-validated way to measure training load without heart-rate monitors. About 30
minutes after a session, the player rates how hard the whole session felt on a 1-10 scale (RPE). Load =
**session duration in minutes x RPE**. For example a 60-minute session rated 7 gives a load of 420 units.
Adding the session loads for the week gives the weekly load. TactiQ calculates this automatically from the
training log.

## Progressing load safely
Large, sudden increases in training load are associated with higher injury risk, while a steadily built-up
higher load can actually be protective. A common way to watch this is the **acute:chronic workload ratio
(ACWR)**: the last 7 days' load divided by the average weekly load of the last 4 weeks. A ratio roughly between
0.8 and 1.3 suggests load is in line with what the player is used to; a ratio above about 1.5 indicates a spike,
and the next sessions should be lighter. ACWR is a rough guide, not a precise injury predictor. A simple rule
of thumb is to increase weekly load gradually rather than doubling it.

## Deload and tapering before tournaments
Every few weeks, a lighter week (for example around 40-60% less volume while keeping some intensity) helps the
body absorb training. In the 5-7 days before an important tournament, reduce training volume while keeping short,
sharp sessions, so the player arrives fresh. Do not try new techniques or new heavy exercises right before a
competition.
